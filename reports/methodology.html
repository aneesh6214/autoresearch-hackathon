Part 1: Conceptual Overview

What Was Optimized

The goal was not to fine-tune a model. The goal was to discover a stronger tutoring system prompt and response architecture for a frontier LLM tutor. The optimized tutor should diagnose what the student is missing, avoid giving away final answers when a productive hint is better, and end with a concrete next question.

The search was intentionally broader than single-prompt optimization, but still controlled for a hackathon run. The research agent could vary three independent variable families:

  1. Response architecture: single-pass, hidden diagnosis before response, critique-and-revise, or related variants.
  2. Tutor policy: the behavioral rules that define how direct, Socratic, concise, safe, and diagnosis-focused the tutor should be.
  3. Hidden planning behavior: private reasoning prompts, self-critique prompts, and model-call budget used before producing the student-facing answer.
Key design choice: interpretability was secondary to search quality. The loop was allowed to behave like black-box optimization as long as the final system remained auditable through saved strategies, transcripts, aggregate scores, and final hidden validation.

High-Level Loop

Research Agent

The research agent reads prior outcomes, proposes mutations or recombinations, and uses benchmark feedback to decide where to search next. It is not shown the private final set.

Evaluation System

Each candidate is run as an actual tutor. A judge scores the transcript for correctness, pedagogy, mistake diagnosis, hint quality, leakage, safety, hallucination, refusals, tone, and cognitive load.

The final selected strategy was a two-call diagnose_then_tutor approach. It privately diagnoses the student's likely mistake, then writes only a natural tutor response with one representation hint and one next question.

Pipeline and Stop Criteria

  1. Create a baseline strategy and evaluate it on the inner-loop split.
  2. Ask the research agent to generate mutations and recombinations from prior candidate results.
  3. Run candidate tutor responses on Modal-backed parallel evaluation jobs.
  4. Judge each transcript with structured rubric scoring and aggregate candidate metrics.
  5. Reject candidates that fail quality, safety, leakage, latency, or budget gates.
  6. Promote the best inner-loop candidates to checkpoint validation.
  7. Run the strongest checkpoint candidate against the private hidden final set.

Selected Tutor Strategy

Response architecture: diagnose_then_tutor

Student-facing policy: first determine the student's likely misconception or missing step, then produce a natural response rather than a formal diagnosis label. Be specific about what the student counted, ignored, reversed, or interpreted too narrowly. Give one representation hint, end with one question, and do not solve the problem.

Hidden planning prompt: diagnose the student's error precisely before writing. Choose the lowest-leakage scaffold: wording check, list setup without entries, unit check, or inclusion/exclusion check.
